macOS says Spectra "is damaged"? It isn't.

Spectra isn't code-signed yet (an Apple Developer certificate is on the roadmap), and today's macOS reports any unsigned download as "damaged and can't be opened" — it offers the Bin instead of asking politely. Your download is fine, and you can prove it with the checksum below. After dragging Spectra into Applications, run this one line in Terminal, then open the app normally:

xattr -cr /Applications/Spectra.app

That clears the quarantine flag on Spectra alone — it touches nothing else and you'll never need it again for this copy. Signed, notarized builds will make this step disappear entirely.

What happens next

From download to X-ray in three steps.

1

Drag to Applications

Open the .dmg and drag Spectra into Applications. That's the whole install — no wizard, no agreement marathon, no account. If macOS calls the app "damaged", it isn't: one xattr command clears it — see the note above.

2

Run your first scan

Point Spectra at your disk and grant Full Disk Access when macOS asks. Under a minute later, every gigabyte has a name.

3

Go Pro — if you want

The free tier is yours forever. When you're ready to reclaim space, Spectra Pro is a one-time $11.99 — unlocked with a key, right in the app.

Requirements

What your Mac needs.

  • macOS 12 Monterey or later — Monterey, Ventura, Sonoma, Sequoia and newer
  • Apple Silicon or Intel — native builds for both; no Rosetta required
  • About 110 MB to download — ironic, we know; Spectra earns it back quickly
  • Full Disk Access (optional but recommended) — lets the X-Ray see everything; granted once in System Settings
  • No internet connection needed — Spectra works fully offline, by design

Verify your download — SHA-256

Paranoia is a feature. After downloading, run shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/Spectra-1.1.0-*.dmg in Terminal and compare it against the checksum published alongside the installer on the v1.1.0 release page — every build is checksummed there when it ships.
